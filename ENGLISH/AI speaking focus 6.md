# 2. Important Corrections

| Original | Natural English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I haven't been here for a long time. | **I haven't talked to you in a long time.** | More natural when referring to a conversation partner. |
| I had lots of businesses. | **I had a lot of things to do.** | *Business* does not mean “tasks/things to do” here. |

| I believe myself. | **I believe in myself.** | Fixed expression. |
| I can express my mind. | **I can express my thoughts / ideas.** | More natural collocation. |
| One point I want you to take in attention. | **One thing I want you to pay attention to.** | Fixed collocation: **pay attention to**. |

# 3. Grammar Review — Must Have / Might Have

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must have + past participle** | Strong deduction about the past | **My son must have bought a new phone.** |
| **might have + past participle** | Past possibility | **My wife might have taken a shower.** |

## Examples from Today

| Situation | Sentence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You see a wet towel. | **My wife might have taken a shower.** | Possible, but not certain. |
| You suddenly see your son active on Telegram. | **My son must have bought a new phone.** | Strong evidence. |
| Half the cake is gone. | **My son must have eaten half the cake.** | Strong deduction. |

## Past Participle Reminder

| Base form | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| buy | bought | **bought** |
| take | took | **taken** |
| eat | ate | **eaten** |
| go | went | **gone** |
| see | saw | **seen** |

# 4. New Grammar — Used To

**used to + base verb** describes a repeated habit or state in the past that is no longer true now.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **used to + verb** | Past habit/state that is no longer true | **I used to play tennis.** |
| **past simple + for + period** | Duration | **I played tennis for two years.** |
| **past simple + specific time** | One completed event | **Last week, I visited a friend.** |

## Important Distinction

- **I used to play tennis.** → past habit, no longer true
- **I played tennis for two years.** → duration
- **I played tennis two years ago.** → when it happened
- <span style="color:red">**I played tennis for two years ago.**</span> → incorrect

# 7. Listening Comprehension — Major Progress

Earlier, the listening process often looked like this:

1. try to catch every word;
2. miss one word;
3. keep thinking about it;
4. miss the next words;
5. lose the whole sentence;
6. become stressed.

Today, the strategy is changing:

1. listen to the whole phrase;
2. accept that some words may be missed;
3. follow the overall meaning;
4. use context;
5. stay relaxed;
6. continue listening.

This is a much more effective real-world listening strategy.

# 9. Pronunciation and Connected Speech

| Written Form | Natural Spoken Chunk | Practice Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **used to** | often sounds close to “yoosta” | **I used to play tennis.** |
| **must have** | often becomes **must’ve** | **He must’ve bought a new phone.** |
| **might have** | often becomes **might’ve** | **She might’ve taken a shower.** |
| **a lot of** | often compressed in fast speech | **I had a lot of things to do.** |
| **pay attention to** | spoken as one rhythmic unit | **Pay attention to the main idea.** |

Recognition comes first. Do not force reductions when speaking.

# Final Reflection

Today’s biggest achievement was not a new grammar rule.

It was the change in listening strategy.

Earlier, missing one word could break the entire sentence. Now, you are increasingly able to let that word go, stay with the speaker, understand the main idea, and continue.

That is exactly the skill needed for real conversations with fluent and native speakers.
