## 5. Grammar — Deductions About the Past

### Form

> **modal + have + past participle**

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must have + V3** | I am almost sure this happened | `My mom must have made the coffee.` |
| **might have + V3** | Maybe this happened | `My wife might have made the coffee.` |
| **can't have + V3** | I am almost sure this did not happen | `My neighbor can't have made it. He doesn't have a key.` |

### Certainty Scale

| Construction | Approximate idea |
|---|---|
| **must have** | very likely |
| **might have** | possible |
| **can't have** | very unlikely / impossible |

### Important Point

You already knew the structure quite well. The difficulty was not grammar itself but quickly inventing a realistic situation for **can't have**.

## 6. Native Speaker Upgrade — `I could've sworn...`

### Meaning

**I could've sworn...** = *I was very sure something was true, but now something makes me doubt it.*

It usually contains a contrast:

**strong memory / belief → new evidence → doubt**

### Compare

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `I was sure I put it there.` | Neutral report about what I believed in the past. It does **not automatically tell us** whether I still believe it. |
| `I could've sworn I put it there.` | I strongly remember putting it there, **but now the situation suggests I may be wrong**. |

### Your real-life example

A very good situation from the lesson:

`I could've sworn I took my driver's license out of the car and put it on the board, but then I couldn't find it.`

This is exactly the kind of situation where a native speaker may use **I could've sworn...**

### Structure

> **I could've sworn + subject + past verb**

Examples:

- `I could've sworn I locked the door.`
- `I could've sworn I left my phone on the desk.`
- `I could've sworn someone knocked on the door.`

### Pronunciation

**could've sworn** → often sounds close to:

`could-uhv sworn`

The main stressed word is usually **sworn**.

## 9. Coffee Topic — Useful Natural English

You described making coffee as something more than simply preparing a drink.

Natural ways to express your idea:

- `Making coffee is almost a ritual for me.`
- `It helps me relax and switch off.`
- `I feel like a barista when I make it.`
- `I enjoy making coffee for my guests.`
- `I put a lot of care into it.`
- `I think food tastes better when you make it in a good state of mind.`

### `savor` vs `taste`

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **taste** | experience the flavour of something |
| **savor** | deliberately enjoy the taste slowly and fully |

Example:

`I tasted the chocolate.` → I experienced its flavour.

`I savored the chocolate.` → I slowly enjoyed the experience of eating it.
