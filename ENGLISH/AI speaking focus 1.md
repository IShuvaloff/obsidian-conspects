# 6. Grammar Focus — Modal Verbs for Deduction

Today we began working with:

- **must have**
    
- **might have**
    
- later we will add **could have** and **can’t have**
    

These structures are used when we make guesses or deductions about the past.

---

## Past Participle

The **past participle** is the third form of a verb.

Examples:

|Base form|Past simple|Past participle|
|---|---|---|
|go|went|gone|
|eat|ate|eaten|
|see|saw|seen|
|fall|fell|fallen|
|come|came|come|
|leave|left|left|
|close|closed|closed|

A simple way to remember it:

**must have + third form**

**might have + third form**

---

# 7. Must Have

## Meaning

Use **must have + past participle** when you are almost sure something happened.

### Example

The child is asleep.

**She must have fallen asleep.**

Meaning:

I am almost certain that she fell asleep.

---

## Another example

The office is empty and everyone’s coats are gone.

**They must have gone home.**

---

# 8. Might Have

## Meaning

Use **might have + past participle** when something is possible, but you are not sure.

### Example

Someone left suddenly.

**They might have left in a hurry.**

Meaning:

Maybe they left in a hurry.

---

# 9. Must Have vs. Might Have

## Must have

High certainty.

**He must have forgotten.**

= I am almost sure he forgot.

## Might have

Possibility.

**He might have forgotten.**

= Maybe he forgot.

---

# 12. Pronunciation and Connected Speech

Today’s useful expressions should be practiced as complete chunks rather than isolated words.

## Catching up

Natural rhythm:

**catching UP**

Example:

**We spent a few hours catching up.**

---

## Put in effort

In natural speech:

**put-in effort**

The words often connect.

---

## Must have

Native speech often reduces:

**must have** → approximately **must've**

Example:

**She must've fallen asleep.**

Do not force the contraction yet. First make the full structure automatic.

---

## Might have

Often pronounced:

**might've**

Example:

**He might've left already.**

Again, accuracy first, reduction later.

---

## The time flew by

Stress:

**TIME** — **FLEW BY**

Say it as one rhythmical phrase:

**The time just flew by.**
