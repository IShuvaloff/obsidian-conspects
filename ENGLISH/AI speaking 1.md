# New Vocabulary

## starving
**Part of speech:** adjective

### Meaning
Very hungry.

This is one of the most common everyday ways to say that you are extremely hungry.

### Examples

- I'm starving.
- I haven't eaten all day. I'm starving.

### Similar expressions

- very hungry
- absolutely hungry (less common)
- hungry as a bear (informal)

### Notes

✅ Native speakers most often say:

> **I'm starving.**

instead of

> I'm very, very hungry.

---

## buckwheat
**Part of speech:** noun (uncountable)

### Meaning

A grain used for making porridge or other dishes.

### Examples

- I usually eat buckwheat for breakfast.
- Buckwheat is very popular in Eastern Europe.

### Collocations

- cook buckwheat
- buckwheat with milk
- buckwheat porridge

---

## porridge
**Part of speech:** noun

### Meaning

A hot breakfast made by cooking grains in water or milk.

### Example

- I had buckwheat porridge with milk.

### Note

Although **buckwheat with milk** is perfectly understandable, if you're describing the finished breakfast dish, you can also say:

> **buckwheat porridge**

# Useful Expressions

## I'm starving.

### Meaning

I'm extremely hungry.

### Example

I'm starving.
Let's have lunch.

---

## I usually have buckwheat with milk for breakfast.

A much more natural sentence than:

> I cook buckwheat with milk.

When talking about your breakfast routine, English usually focuses on **eating** rather than **cooking**.

---

## I can't picture...

### Meaning

To find it difficult to imagine something.

### Examples

- I can't picture my life without coffee.
- I can't picture working anywhere else.

### Similar expressions

- I can't imagine...
- I can't see myself...

---

## I can't see myself...

### Meaning

A very common way to say that you cannot imagine yourself doing something.

### Examples

- I can't see myself living in another country.
- I can't see myself changing careers.

### Notes

This is more conversational than:

> I can't imagine...

---

## someday

### Meaning

At some time in the future.

### Examples

- I hope someday I can work for an international company.
- Someday I'd like to travel to Japan.

---

## international company

A more natural phrase than:

> foreign company

### Example

I hope someday I can work for an international company.

---

## get through an interview

### Meaning

To successfully complete an interview.

### Examples

- I hope I can get through the interview.
- She got through all three interview stages.

### Similar expressions

- pass the interview
- succeed in the interview

---

## practice for interviews

Natural expression.

### Examples

- I'd like to practice for interviews.
- Could you help me practice for technical interviews?

---

## mock interview

### Meaning

A practice interview.

### Examples

- Let's do a mock interview.
- Mock interviews really improve confidence.

# Grammar Corrections

## ❌ I'm hungry like a dog.

✅ I'm starving.

(Much more common.)

---

## ✅ I'm hungry as a bear.

Grammatically correct.

Native speakers understand it, but **I'm starving** is far more common.

---

## ❌ I cook buckwheat with milk every morning.

Better:

✅ I usually have buckwheat with milk for breakfast.

---

## ❌ I hope someday I can get through the interview to a foreign company.

Better:

✅ I hope someday I can get through an interview at an international company.

Even more natural:

✅ I hope someday I can get a job at an international company.

---

## ❌ Could you train me interviews?

Better:

✅ Could you help me practice for interviews?

or

✅ Could we do a mock interview?
